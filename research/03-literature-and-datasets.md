# Landscape Research 03 — NLP Literature & Datasets for Scientific Writing Assistance

*Researchly project · Landscape research phase · Compiled 2026-08-10.*
*Focus: what the research literature offers per capability area, and what is reusable (data, models, task formulations, evaluation methods). Sources at the end.*

---

## 1. Grammatical Error Correction (GEC)

**State of the art.** Three paradigm families coexist: (a) sequence tagging — **GECToR** (Omelianchuk et al., BEA 2020), GEC as token-level edit tagging ("tag, not rewrite"), fast and still competitive, official Grammarly code on GitHub; (b) seq2seq — T5/Flan-T5-based rewriting (Rothe et al. 2021 lineage); (c) LLM-based GEC — zero/few-shot frontier models and instruction-tuned open LLMs. The definitive recent synthesis is **"Pillars of Grammatical Error Correction"** (Omelianchuk et al., BEA 2024, Grammarly): single-model SOTA on CoNLL-2014 and BEA-2019 comes from ensembles/ranking combinations of taggers, seq2seq, and LLMs. Key finding: **LLMs alone over-correct** (high recall, fluency-style rewrites), scoring *lower* on minimal-edit metrics but *higher* on human fluency preference. "ChatGPT or Grammarly?" (Wu et al., 2023) and "Adapting LLMs for Minimal-edit GEC" (2025) confirm this precision/fluency trade-off.

**Benchmarks.** **CoNLL-2014** (M2 scorer, F0.5, minimal edits); **BEA-2019 W&I+LOCNESS** (ERRANT-based F0.5; current standard); **JFLEG** (Napoles et al., EACL 2017; fluency-oriented, GLEU — the evaluation philosophy closest to an LLM-era assistant).

**AESW 2016** (Daudaravicius et al., BEA @ NAACL 2016) is the single most Researchly-relevant GEC resource: **~1.2M sentences from ~9,900 published journal articles** professionally edited by VTeX language editors, with before/after edits; tasks were (1) binary "does this sentence need editing" and (2) probabilistic scoring. Distributed from textmining.lt/aesw. Exactly the "scientific register" GEC distribution Researchly needs — but licensing must be re-verified (distribution was task-bound) and the domain skews physics/math/CS.

**Reuse:** copy AESW's "needs-edit detection + minimal edit" pipeline; evaluate with ERRANT F0.5 for precision-critical inline suggestions and GLEU/human preference for fluency rewrites. GECToR is Apache-licensed and production-viable; LLM GEC needs an over-correction guard (minimal-edit constrained decoding or post-filtering, per the Pillars paper).

## 2. Rhetorical Structure of Scientific Text

- **Argumentative Zoning** (Teufel 1999; Teufel & Moens 2002, *Computational Linguistics*): sentences labeled AIM/BACKGROUND/OWN/CONTRAST/BASIS etc.; AZ corpus from Teufel's Cambridge page; AZ-II extended to chemistry. Foundational formulation of "what rhetorical job is this sentence doing."
- **CARS/move analysis automation:** Anthony & Lashkia's *Mover* (2003) began this; Cotos's **Research Writing Tutor** (Iowa State) operationalized Swales moves as pedagogical feedback across 30+ disciplines; a 2025 *Electronics* paper ("Automatic Detection of the CaRS Framework in Scholarly Writing Using NLP") shows modern transformer/LLM classification of CARS moves/steps in introductions, with a circulating annotation manual (USI). Accuracy is decent at move level, weaker at step level — feasible as soft guidance, not hard validation.
- **IMRaD/sentence-role classification:** Agarwal & Yu (*Bioinformatics* 2009) for full-text biomedical sentences; **PubMed 200k RCT** (Dernoncourt & Lee, IJCNLP 2017) — 200k abstracts with sentences labeled BACKGROUND/OBJECTIVE/METHODS/RESULTS/CONCLUSIONS, public on GitHub, the workhorse dataset (hierarchical LSTM baselines ~92% F1; trivially beaten by modern encoders). **CSAbstruct** (Cohan et al., EMNLP 2019, with SciBERT) covers CS abstracts. Brack et al. (2024, *IJDL*): cross-domain multi-task sequential sentence classification generalizes across corpora.
- **CODA/ART corpus** (Liakata et al., LREC 2010; *Bioinformatics* 2012): 265 physical-chemistry/biochemistry papers annotated with 11 **Core Scientific Concepts** (Hypothesis, Motivation, Goal, Method, Result, Conclusion…); automatic CoreSC recognition demonstrated.

**Reuse:** section/move awareness is the most mature capability. PubMed RCT + CSAbstruct give near-free training data for abstract-role detection; AZ/CoreSC/ART are better used as few-shot LLM prompt schemas than training sets (small, older corpora). Cotos's RWT is the proof that move feedback is pedagogically valuable.

## 3. Hedging / Certainty / Exaggeration

- **BioScope corpus** (Vincze et al., 2008): ~20k biomedical sentences annotated for negation/speculation cues and scopes. **CoNLL-2010 shared task** (Farkas et al.): hedge cue detection + scope resolution — the classic benchmark; near-solved at cue level with modern encoders.
- **Certainty measurement:** **Pei & Jurgens (EMNLP 2021)**, "Measuring Sentence-Level and Aspect-Level (Un)certainty in Science Communications" — regression model of certainty plus aspect-level annotation (certainty about number, extent, probability…); released the `certainty-estimator` pip package and data. **Directly reusable for a "hedging meter."**
- **Exaggeration/spin:** Sumner et al. (BMJ 2014) established press-release exaggeration empirically; **Wright & Augenstein (EMNLP 2021)** formalized exaggeration detection as NLP with an abstract-vs-press-release benchmark (copenlu GitHub); Yu et al. (COLING 2020) measured correlation-to-causation exaggeration; **Yu, Wang & Yu (EMNLP 2019) "Detecting Causal Language Use in Science Findings"** classifies claim strength (direct causal / conditional causal / correlational / no relation) in PubMed conclusions — a ready-made 4-way claim-strength scheme.
- **Spin in RCT abstracts:** Boutron's group defined spin taxonomies; **DeSpin** (Koroleva et al., BioNLP 2020) is a prototype automated spin detector; a 2025 JMIR paper shows GPT-4-class models detecting spin in oncology RCT abstracts with careful prompting — promising, not yet reliable enough for autonomous flagging.
- **Observational-study causal language:** *Am J Epidemiol* 2022 ("Causal and Associational Language in Observational Health Research") quantified widespread mismatch between methods and causal wording, with annotated data and causal-verb word lists; arXiv 2502.12159 (2025) scales this with NLP over large corpora.

**Reuse:** this is Researchly's differentiator area with the best data-to-ambition ratio. Concrete stack: BioScope/CoNLL-2010 for cue detection; Pei & Jurgens for graded certainty; Yu et al. 2019 for causal-claim strength; Wright & Augenstein for claim-vs-evidence mismatch. All have public data/code. The hard part is calibration *against the study design* (does the hedge level fit the evidence?), which needs methods-section understanding — research-grade.

## 4. Statistical & Causal Language Checking

- **statcheck** (Nuijten & Epskamp; Nuijten et al. 2020, *Res Synth Methods*): open-source R package that re-computes p-values from APA-style test statistics; validity studies show large agreement with manual checks; Nuijten & Wicherts (AMPPS 2024) showed statcheck-in-peer-review at *Psychological Science* caused a steep decline in reporting inconsistencies — strong evidence automated checks change author behavior. GitHub: MicheleNuijten/statcheck. Limitation: APA-format-dependent; an LLM-based extractor could generalize it.
- **Reporting-guideline compliance:** **CONSORT-TM** (Kilicoglu et al., 2024): corpus of RCT publications annotated at sentence level against 37 fine-grained CONSORT items, with BioBERT and GPT-4 baselines (fine-tuned PubMedBERT beats zero-shot GPT-4); extended to **SPIRIT-CONSORT-TM** (*Scientific Data* 2025) covering protocols. This is the template for "checklist coverage" feedback. **No equivalent mature STROBE corpus exists — a gap Researchly could fill; EPIFORGE (Pollett et al., PLOS Med 2021, 19 items for epidemic forecasting papers) has no automation at all — an EPIFORGE checker would be first-of-kind.**
- **RobotReviewer** (Marshall, Kuiper & Wallace): ML extraction of PICO and risk-of-bias judgments from trial PDFs; open source; Cochrane trials showed human+machine workflows work. An architectural precedent for document-level appraisal.
- **Causal-claim appropriateness:** combine the Yu et al. 2019 claim-strength classifier with a study-design detector (RCT vs observational) to flag "observational design + causal verb" mismatches. The AJE 2022 paper provides annotated seed data and causal-verb word lists ("affect," "reduce," "increase").

**Reuse:** statcheck is reusable today (wrap it; extend with LLM extraction of non-APA stats). CONSORT-TM data + models are public and fine-tunable. Design-vs-language mismatch detection is a near-term, high-value, low-competition feature.

## 5. LLM Writing Assistance Research

- **Foundational co-writing systems:** **CoAuthor** (Lee, Liang & Yang, CHI 2022) — dataset of 1,445 GPT-3 co-writing sessions with full keystroke/suggestion logs (public; excellent for interaction design and evaluation methodology); **Wordcraft** (Google). Newer: **ABScribe** (CHI 2024, managing multiple AI variations); "Shaping Human-AI Collaboration: Varied Scaffolding Levels" (CHI 2024 — *more* AI scaffolding isn't monotonically better); "Co-Writing with AI, on Human Terms" (PACM HCI 2025) — maps user demands across the writing process; a useful requirements framework.
- **LLM feedback on papers:** **Liang et al. 2023** (arXiv 2310.01783, later *NEJM AI*): GPT-4 review feedback on Nature-family + ICLR papers overlapped with human reviewers about as much as reviewers overlap with each other; >50% of surveyed authors found it helpful; code public. **ICLR 2025 randomized study of 20K reviews**: LLM feedback *on reviews* improved review quality. **MARG** (D'Arcy et al. 2024) improves specificity of generated reviews via multi-agent decomposition. LLM-as-reviewer benchmarks (PRISM, MMReview, 2025–2026) consistently find: good at surface/clarity comments, weak at novelty/soundness, vulnerable to prompt injection.
- **Focused feedback for scientific writing:** **SWIF²T** ("Automated Focused Feedback Generation for Scientific Writing Assistance," ACL Findings 2024) — plans and generates specific, actionable critiques of weaknesses in scientific text; directly on-target for Researchly. Outcome studies: "Accepted with Minor Revisions" (2025); *Contemporary Ed Psych* 2026 on LLM feedback effects on student writing performance.
- **Revision data:** **ARIES** (D'Arcy et al., ACL 2024, allenai/aries): 196 human-labeled + 3.9k auto-aligned reviewer-comment→paper-edit pairs from OpenReview; GPT-4 struggled to generate appropriate edits (overly literal/generic). The key dataset for a "response to reviewers" assistant. Also: CASIMIR, Re³ revision corpora; **AcademiCraft** (2025, multi-agent EAP writing assistance).
- **Style/register:** formality transfer is mature on GYAFC (Rao & Tetreault 2018) but general-domain; academic-register-specific transfer is thin. Fine-tuning on AESW edit pairs is arguably a better route to "scientific register transfer" than GYAFC.

**Reuse:** adopt Liang et al.'s evaluation method (overlap with human reviewer comments + author-perceived helpfulness) as Researchly's north-star eval. ARIES gives the response-to-reviewers task off the shelf. CoAuthor's logging schema is the template for collecting Researchly's own interaction data.

## 6. Personalization / Explainability of Feedback

- **AWE tradition:** ETS **e-rater** (Attali & Burstein 2006) and **Criterion** pioneered trait-level explainable scoring (grammar, usage, mechanics, style, organization/development); **Writing Mentor** (ETS, Google-Docs add-in) delivers pedagogically framed, non-judgmental feedback on coherence, claims/evidence, and convention use. The AIED/Springer "Automated Scoring of Writing" and "Future of Intelligent Tutoring Systems for Writing" chapters (2023) survey the move from scores to formative feedback.
- **Research Writing Tutor** (Cotos): discipline-specific, move-based feedback with corpus-drawn exemplars — the strongest existing model of "explainable, convention-grounded" scientific-writing feedback.
- **Personalization:** Kochmar et al. (AIED 2020) — cleanest experimental evidence that personalized feedback improves learning gains; *Computers & Education: AI* 2022 review provides a classification framework. AWE syntheses (e.g., *System* 2024) show learners often ignore or misapply generic feedback — motivation for level-adaptive explanation. **Research on adapting scientific-writing feedback to author expertise is essentially nonexistent — open territory.**
- **Explainability angle:** corpus-evidence-backed suggestions ("in 20k PubMed discussion sections, this construction appears with frequency X") have precedent in RWT and data-driven learner tools (Ludwig, SkELL) but no rigorous evaluation exists.

**Reuse:** adopt e-rater's trait decomposition and Writing Mentor's feedback framing; combine with RWT's move-exemplar approach. The pedagogy literature says: show *why* (rule/convention + corpus evidence + exemplar) first, offer the fix second.

## Implications for Researchly

**Near-term feasible (mostly assembly of public components):**

1. Scientific-register GEC: GECToR/T5 + LLM ensemble, minimal-edit constrained, fine-tuned/evaluated on AESW-style edit pairs; ERRANT + human-preference eval.
2. Section/move awareness: abstract sentence-role tagging (PubMed RCT, CSAbstruct); introduction move detection (CARS via few-shot LLM + RWT-style feedback); IMRaD detection is trivial.
3. Hedge/certainty meter: BioScope cues + Pei-Jurgens certainty estimator, surfaced as per-section calibration feedback.
4. statcheck integration + LLM-generalized stat extraction; p-value/df consistency checks.
5. Causal-wording vs study-design mismatch flags (Yu et al. classifier + design detector + AJE word lists).
6. CONSORT checklist coverage using CONSORT-TM models; response-to-reviewers drafting grounded in ARIES.

**Research-grade hard (differentiating bets):**

- Evidence-calibrated hedging: judging whether *claim strength fits the evidence in the paper* (needs methods+results understanding; spin detection is prototype-level even with GPT-4-class models).
- STROBE/EPIFORGE automation: no corpora exist — Researchly would need to annotate (CONSORT-TM's methodology is the recipe; an EPIFORGE checker would be first-of-kind and publishable).
- Discipline-conditioned convention models at scale (beyond RWT's hand-built corpora).
- Personalization to author level with demonstrated learning gains — no prior art in scientific writing specifically; requires longitudinal user data (CoAuthor-style logging from day one).
- Reliable LLM review of substance (novelty/soundness): current benchmarks say no — scope Researchly's claims to clarity, reporting, and rhetoric, not scientific merit.

**Cross-cutting eval strategy:** minimal-edit F0.5 for corrections; sentence-role accuracy vs PubMed-RCT; overlap-with-human-reviewer + author helpfulness ratings (Liang et al. protocol); behavior-change metrics à la statcheck-at-Psych-Science.
