# Researchly Knowledge Base — Sources and Where They Live in the App

*2026-08-10 · Maps every document in `Notes_on_writing/` (plus the original
writing guide) to the principles mined from it and the concrete place each
principle now lives in Researchly: a rule id, a lexicon, an explanation
text, or the roadmap. This is the provenance record — every rule can point
back to the lecture or book that taught it.*

The organizing idea, taken from the NG5001 course: **writing is a dialogue
between writer and (imagined) reader**. The checker plays the reader's
side — a friendly-sceptical reader asking Wallace & Wray's question, "Have
you given me sufficient grounds for accepting your claim?" — and every
suggestion is that reader speaking with a named reason.

## 1. Dr Akira Endo — "Scientific writing for infectious disease modelling" (`writing.pdf`)

The single most rule-productive document: field-specific, experience-based,
and almost every tip is checkable. Seven new rules (E-series) plus lexicon
and explanation upgrades:

| Endo's principle | Where it lives now |
|---|---|
| Information density: minimise "empty" sentences ("holistic understanding of complex dynamics") | **E701 empty-phrase** (+ `EMPTY_PHRASES` lexicon) |
| Adversative overuse ("However… However…") | **E702 adversative-run** |
| "This/that/it — what does it refer to?" (ambiguous after long sentences) | **E703 naked-this** (fires only after long preceding sentences, per his own caveat) |
| Facts vs opinions: "is known/considered to be" — who says? cite it | **E704 uncited-consensus** (quiet when a citation is present) |
| "X has not been done — don't take the risk" | **E705 absolute-novelty** (quiet with "to our knowledge") |
| Implicit comparator: "relatively — compared with what?" | **E706 missing-comparator** |
| Results narrative: don't open with "Fig 1 shows…"; lead with the finding | **E707 display-first-opener** (Results only) |
| Bombastic verbs: utilise/employ/leverage/elucidate → use/show | **W201 lexicon extended** (leverage, elucidate added) |
| "in terms of / in light of / in relation to" | **W209** (existing; Endo corroborates) |
| Be humble as a modeller: "children contributed most to flu transmission" NOT OK without "in our model" | **C303's why-text** already carries this; full model-claims check = C307 (review-lens, planned) |
| Therefore/thus require a real logical link | inventory **D-layer** (review lens): logic verification isn't deterministic |
| Different terms = different things; don't fear repetition | **W210/F605 terminology consistency** (v-next priority, now with two independent sources) |
| Leaps of logic; readers have read only what they've read; define at first use | **F601/F602** (acronyms shipped; term-definition = review lens) |
| Figure self-sufficiency, caption "sufficient not necessary", y-axis at 0 | **F611** (review-lens, planned) + future figure checks |
| Intro = Context → Issues → Current status/gap → What you do | **D501/D902** structure model (D902 shipped) |
| Cover letter ≠ abstract; grant = pitch to outsiders; examples beat logic for non-experts | knowledge base only — future "document type: cover letter/grant" modes (matches project brief's context list) |

## 2. NG5001 Unit 1 — "Reading Critically" (`Unit 1 - 2510.pdf`)

The reader's-side framework. Mostly powers explanations and the review
lens, plus one rule:

- **Argument = conclusion + warranting** (Wallace & Wray): the frame behind
  the whole C-series; C303/C302/E704 messages ask the warranting question.
- **The serial-summary literature review** (the KM example: "There are many
  studies dealing with… A claimed… B claimed… By the way…") →
  **L901 serial-summary-opener**, and the review-as-conversation framing in
  its explanation.
- Niche-establishment strategies list (12 ways to establish, 6 ways to
  emphasize) → knowledge for the **D-layer review lens**.
- Critical-reader checklist ("check how authors define key terms and
  whether they are consistent") → corroborates **W210/F605**.

## 3. NG5001 Unit 2 — "Writing Academically" (`Unit 2 - 2510.pdf`)

- **Metadiscourse (Hyland) + coherence**: old-before-new information
  structure (already the known–new contract in the guide); interactive/
  interactional resources give the vocabulary for a future
  **metadiscourse meter** (hedges, boosters, attitude markers, engagement)
  — natural extension of the C301/C305 machinery + M901 metrics.
- **Voice (Wellington et al.)**: "confidence without cockiness, scepticism
  without cynicism" — the calibration philosophy behind C301/C302/C305,
  now quotable in their explanations.
- **Thomas (2016) style checklist** (long sentences, abstract-noun overuse,
  redundancy, vagueness, gratuitous jargon) → independently corroborates
  G103/G106/W206/W202 — added as source attributions in the inventory.
- **Conference abstract structure (Belcher/SciQua + Wallace & Wray focus
  questions)** → **AB801 vague-forward-reference** (shipped) and the
  planned **abstract review lens**: 6-part completeness check (conventional
  wisdom → problematization → question → methods → findings →
  significance) with Pallas's sentence frames as constructive suggestions.
- **Common ground for cross-disciplinary readers** (define jargon, shared
  mental models, examples) → F601 (shipped), F602 (planned), and the
  persona note in the knowledge base.

## 4. Wallace & Wray ch. 4 — "Getting Started on Critical Reading" (`…critical_reading.pdf`)

The five **Critical Synopsis Questions** (Why am I reading this? What are
the authors trying to achieve? What are they claiming? How convincing?
What use can I make of it?) and the **argument-flaw taxonomy** (conclusion
without warranting, warranting without conclusion, illogical conclusion,
unlinked warranting, inadequate warranting).

Where it lives: this is the specification for the **document-level review
lens** — the on-demand "critical reader" pass planned since the synthesis
(research/04's coaching pattern). The flaw taxonomy maps directly onto the
Toulmin audit (C308–C310 in the inventory). The warranting-indicator words
(therefore, because, since, it follows that) join Endo's therefore/thus
observation as the anchor points that pass will inspect. Also the origin of
E704's demand: "demand a convincing warranting for every conclusion."

## 5. Shehzad 2008 — "Move two: establishing a niche" (`03_15_Shehzad (1).pdf` = `Week 2 Reading.pdf`)

The corpus-based anatomy of gap statements:

- **Linguistic indicators of gaps** — contrastives (however, while, but,
  although, nevertheless, as opposed to, with few exceptions), quantifiers/
  quasi-negatives (limited, few, little), negatives (none of, not been, no
  work/research/data/study) → the detection lexicon inside **D902
  missing-niche-signal** (shipped): if an Introduction contains no
  contrastive + gap-word signal, Researchly asks "where is Move 2?"
- Gap-statement types (counter-claiming, gap-indicating, question-raising,
  tradition-continuing) → review-lens taxonomy for D501/D502.
- "Move 2 is the hinge… builds up a demand for the current contribution" →
  D902's explanation text.

## 6. Pallas 2023, *Doctoral Student Skills* (`Doctoral Student Skills….pdf`; ch. 7 duplicated as `Writing conference abstract.pdf`)

- **The 6-part abstract structure + fill-in sentence frames** → AB801's
  rationale + the planned abstract lens; the frames belong in a future
  "abstract scaffold" feature (insert-template with prompts).
- Abstract length pragmatics (match the call; don't come in far under) →
  future doc-level metric ("abstract is 180 words against a 500-word
  limit — reviewers asked for detail").
- Conference-abstract-specific wisdom (new/interesting/focused/informative/
  understandable/enticingly-titled) → knowledge base for the abstract lens.

## 7. Week 2 & Week 4 Key Points (`Week 2/4 Key Points.pdf`)

- Warranting differs by discipline/paradigm (positivist/interpretivist/

## 8. Understanding Research Paradigm (Omodan 2025)

Context document: paradigms (positivism/interpretivism/critical/pragmatism)
shape what counts as warranting. No deterministic rule — informs the
discipline-conditioned convention packs and reviewer-lens framing. Recorded
so the epistemology behind "different disciplines warrant differently"
has a citable source in the knowledge base.

## 11. The discourse tier: authored questions behind the narrative map (S4, 2026-10-08)

`researchly/discourse/moves.py`. Each move a section is expected to make is an authored question with a named source, a lesson in everyday words and a fill-in frame in Researchly's own wording (curated text, never generated; the idea of one frame per move is Pallas's, her frames are not reproduced). Sources are cited by name on the map's source line; the questions, lessons and frames name none of them.

| Section | Moves (in order) | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract | what is known, what is missing, what you set out to do, how it was done, what was found, why it matters now | the six-move abstract (the course's Unit 2, shown as the owner's learnings); guide §2 |
| Introduction | why it matters, what is missing, what you set out to do | Swales (1990) CARS; Shehzad (2008) on the niche; guide §3 |
| Methods | the data, the analysis | guide §2 |
| Results | what was found, the evidence | guide §9.7 |
| Discussion | the main finding restated, what it means, against earlier work, what this cannot show, what should change | guide §2 (OCAR, the hourglass), §4 (warranting), §7.3; Wallace & Wray; Toulmin (1958); guide §9.8 |
| Conclusion | what this adds, what should change | Wallace & Wray; guide §2, §3, §9.8 |

Order is judged in the abstract and the introduction only. The argument's missing links (`discourse/argument.py`: claim without warrant, claim without grounds, evidence without interpretation, limitation not reflected in claim strength, contribution unclear) cite Wallace & Wray, Toulmin and guide §4, and appear on the map for the argued sections (abstract, introduction, discussion, limitations, conclusion). The hedging trajectory reuses the hedge and booster lexicons of the metrics read-out (Hyland) per section.

## 12. Learn cards and the epidemiology pack (S4c, 2026-10-08)

**Learn cards** (`researchly/learn.py`, exported to `packages/contract/learn.json`). 26 short lessons in six groups (sentences; words; claims and evidence; figures, tables and numbers; structure and argument; mechanics). Each card has a summary, a lesson in everyday words, a before-and-after pair written for Researchly (synthetic, never quoted from a source), a habit to keep, and the source line expanded by `sources.cite()`. Every rule, every narrative move and every argument link carries a `learn_ref` that resolves to a card; a test fails if one does not, and another fails if `learn.json` is out of date. The lessons paraphrase the guide (sentence mechanics after Gopen & Swan, words, calibration, structure, field conventions), Wallace & Wray and Toulmin for claims and warrants, Swales and Shehzad for introductions, and the course material, shown as the owner's learnings. No card names a section number, the course or the lecturer.

**Epidemiology pack** (`researchly/packs/epidemiology.py`). Four reporting checklists, chosen by the writer or suggested from the text (PRISMA, then CONSORT, then EPIFORGE, then STROBE):

| Checklist | Design | Items checked | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| STROBE | observational studies | 16 | von Elm et al. (2007), the STROBE statement |
| CONSORT | randomised trials | 18 | Schulz et al. (2010), CONSORT 2010 |
| PRISMA | systematic reviews | 15 | Page et al. (2021), PRISMA 2020 |
| EPIFORGE | epidemic forecasts and projections | 15 | Pollett et al. (2021), EPIFORGE 2020 |

Each item is paraphrased in Researchly's words, with the section it belongs in and a cue the engine looks for. An item is reported as "reported" (with the sentence that matched) or "needs your check". It is never reported as missing, because a cue cannot prove absence. The pack checks reporting, never the science (P3). The guideline texts are not reproduced; each item cites its statement by name.
